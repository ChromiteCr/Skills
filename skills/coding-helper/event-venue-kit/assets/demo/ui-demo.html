Citations such as this one1 open the folded source list before the page jumps, so a link never lands on a closed box.

Rows of prose keep the measure of the page, and the head of each part becomes a sticky left column on a wide screen. Orbital Kitchen cooks with a solar oven on the quay: every plate at the stall in Pier Nine Hall is ready in the time the sun takes to cross one window.

The queue stays short because each plate takes three minutes. Tasting plates are free until noon; after that a plate costs one token from the fair desk.

02The kitchen厨房
PantryTest stoveTasting counter

Pantry

Salt, oats and dried seaweed come from the harbour market two streets away. The shelves are labelled in two languages, so a visitor can read what is in each jar without asking.

Nothing is bought in plastic; the jars go back to the market every Friday and come home full.

Test stove

The test stove is where new recipes are tried before the fair. Visitors may watch from the rope line while the cook explains each step aloud.

A recipe that fails twice is retired, and the two failed plates are pinned to the wall next to the stove.

Tasting counter

The counter seats six. Each seat has a card with the dish name, the cook's name and the minutes the plate took.

The card asks one question at the bottom: would you cook this at home? The answers are counted at closing time.

03Visit到访

Orbital Kitchen is in Pier Nine Hall, Hall 02. From the main door it is about forty metres: pass the Kelp & Co. stall and keep straight on.

The hall is step-free, and a lift stands beside the stairs at the west end.

Doors open at ten and the last plate is served at four.
